# Dialogue, acting, eyes and takes

## Order of work for an acting shot
1. Listen to the line; mark **accents** (stressed syllables / beats) and **phrases**.
2. Decide the **thought changes** — each change of thought gets a new pose, a blink or
   an eye-dart, and usually a head move.
3. Block the **body** poses on the phrase starts and accents.
4. Add **head/eyes** (eyes lead by 2–4 frames; head follows; shoulders follow that).
5. **Mouth last.**

## Timing dialogue
- The body/head hit lands **2 frames before** the sound of the accented syllable
  (the audience reads motion ahead of sound). Never after.
- Hold through unstressed words with **moving holds**.
- One gesture per phrase, not per word. Gestures *start* before the words they go with.
- Change pose on **thought**, not on syllable.

## Mouth shapes
Use a handful: closed (M/B/P), open (A/E/I vowels), wide (E), round (O/W/OO), teeth (F/V,
S/Z), tongue (L/TH). Rules:
- Hit **M/B/P closed** shapes exactly on the frame — these are the only hard syncs.
- Hold vowels; skip consonants between vowels when fast.
- Shapes change on the accents; do not flap every frame.
- Jaw opens with the stressed vowel and leads the lips by a frame.
- The whole head participates: slight head drop on open vowels, tiny lift on closed.

## Eyes
- **Eyes lead** every look and every head turn (2–4 frames before the head).
- **Blink** on: head turns, thought changes, direction changes, and roughly every 2–4
  seconds of idle time. Blink = close in 2 frames, open in 3–4; often asymmetric
  (one eye a frame late). Blink *during* the fast part of the head turn hides the pop.
- Eye darts: 1–2 frame jumps with a 4–8 frame hold between. Never spline eye darts.
- Pupils/lids: lids follow the eye direction (looking down → upper lids drop).

## Takes (reaction)
```
frame  0     anticipation: eyes close/squint, head drops or pulls back, body sinks (2-4 f)
frame  3     POP: head/eyes snap up and out PAST the final pose, stretched, brows up (1-2 f)
frame  5     settle: ease back into the held reaction pose (4-8 f), overlap on hair/ears
```
Scale the whole thing: subtle double-take in 6 frames; cartoon take in 16+ with a
squash-stretch body.

## Accents and phrasing — the musical idea
Williams treats a performance like a drum part: **accents** are where energy goes, the rest
is **phrasing** between them. Vary accent size (big-small-small-big). Give each phrase a
single arc of energy: build → accent → release. Two accents the same size in a row read dead.

## Acting checklist
- Who is the character thinking about? Where does the eye go *first*?
- Is there contrast between poses (big/small, open/closed, left/right)?
- Does every gesture start before its word and finish after?
- Is anything moving that shouldn't be (noise), or frozen that should drift (dead)?
